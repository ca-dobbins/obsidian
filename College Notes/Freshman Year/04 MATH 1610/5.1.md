### Area of Polygons
- Triangle: $\frac{1}{2}bh$
- Quadrilateral: $A_{1}+A_{2}$, where $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are triangles, called triangularization.

Polygons are easy to find the area, curved regions, however, are not. But we can estimate the area using polygons. Triangles are hard :(, let's use rectangles instead.
### Rectangles
Estimate the shaded area of the function:

$$
f(x)=x^{2}\;[0,1]
$$

Using four rectangles and right endpoints of the curve, we find that...

$$ \Large
\begin{array}{}
R_{4}=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4} \\
=\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(1)^{2} \\
=\frac{15}{32}
\end{array}
$$

But this is still an overestimation. Let's use the left endpoints instead.

$$ \Large
\begin{array}{}
L_{4}=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4} \\
=\frac{1}{4}(0)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{2} \\
=\frac{7}{32}
\end{array}
$$

This is too small, however we can conclude that $\frac{7}{32} <A< \frac{15}{32}$. We can however improve this estimate by using smaller and smaller rectangles. 

$$
R_{8}=\sum_{i=1}^{8} \frac{1}{8}\left( \frac{i}{8} \right)^{2}=.3984375\dots
$$

Still too big, but $A<R_{8}<R_{4}$.

$$
L_{8}=\sum_{i=1}^{7} \frac{1}{8}\left( \frac{i}{8} \right)^{2}=.2734\dots
$$

Still too small, but $L_{4}<L_{8}<A$. So lets use wayyyyy more rectangles... So it appears that

$$
A=\lim_{ n \to \infty } L_{n}=\lim_{ n \to \infty } R_{n}=\frac{1}{3}
$$

So the area $S$ under a positive continuous function $f$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is the limit of the sum of the approximating rectangles.

$$
S=\lim_{ n \to \infty } \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_{i})\Delta x
$$

Where $\Delta x=\frac{b-a}{n}$. This is known as the *Reimann Sum*.
