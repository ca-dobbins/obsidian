### Part II
If the function $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, then,

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx =F(x)|_{a}^b=F(b)-F(a)
$$

where $F(x)$ is any antiderivative of $f(x)$
##### Example(s):

$$
\int_{0}^{1} x^{2} \, dx =\frac{1}{3}x^{3}|_{0}^1=\frac{1}{3}
$$


$$
\int_{1}^{4} e^x \, dx =e^x|_{1}^4=e^4-e^1
$$
### Part I
If $f$ is a continuous function on the closed interval $[a,b]$, then 

$$
g(x)=\int_{a}^{x} f(t) \, dt
$$ 

where $a\leq x\leq b$ is also continuous on the interval $[a,b]$, and it is differentiable on $(a,b)$. Further, $g'(x)=f'(x)$.

Recall [[5.3#Part II|Part II]].

$$
\int_{a}^{x} f(t) \, dt =F(t)|_{a}^x=F(x)-F(a)
$$

So,

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_{a}^{x} f(t) \, dt =\frac{d}{dx}(F(x)-F(a))\to f(x)  
$$

Part I is just another derivative rule, so the chain rule applies. Part I also only works if it is in the form:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_{a}^{x} f(t) \, dt  
$$

##### Example(s):

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_{0}^{x} \sec ^{2}t \, dt =\frac{d}{dx}\tan t|_{0}^x\to \sec ^{2}x  
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx} \int_{1}^{x} e^{t^{2}} \, dt =e^{t^{2}}
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_{0}^{x^{2}} \sin t \, dt =2x\sin x^{2} 
$$
