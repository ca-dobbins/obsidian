### Definition
[giant ahh text block]

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx =\lim_{ x \to \infty } \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_{i}^*)\Delta x
$$

Note: $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx$ is a number, so the variable here does not matter:

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx =\int_{a}^{b} f(z) \, dz =\int_{a}^{b} f(t) \, dt
$$
### "Area"
The definite integral finds the "area" under the curve. This only hold true if the function is positive.

$$
A=\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx
$$

If $f(x)$ has negative values, then the definite integral can produce negative results. However this total area under a curve is the positive regions plus the absolute values of the negative regions. 

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx <0, \; A=A_{1}+|A_{2}|
$$

$A=A_{1}-A_{2}$ is the net area where $A_{1}$ is the total region above the x-axis, and $A_{2}$ is the total region below.
##### Example(s):
Express as an integral:

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_{i}^{3}+x_{i}\cos x_{i})\Delta x\;[0,\pi]
$$


$$
=\int_{0}^{\pi} x^{3}+x\cos x \, dx
$$
