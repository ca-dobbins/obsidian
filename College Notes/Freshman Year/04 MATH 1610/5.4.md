### Notation
***Def***: The indefinite integral of $f(x)$ is:

$$
\int f(x)\,dx = F(x)+C
$$

where $F(x)+C$ is the most general antiderivative of $f(x)$.

The same rules in [[4.9]] apply here.
##### Example(s):

$$
\int \cos x\,dx=\sin x+C
$$

$$
\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C
$$
### Net Change Theorem
The [[5.2#Definition|Definite Integral]] of a rate of change produces net change:

$$
\int_{a}^{b} f'(t) \, dt =f(b)-f(a)
$$

Recall the velocity/position problem:

$$
\int_{a}^{b} v(t) \, dt =s(b)-s(a)
$$

This $s(b)-s(a)$ is called displacement. However displacement and total distance traveled are not (necessarily) the same.
- net change (displacement): $\int_{a}^{b} f(t) \, dt$
- total distance: $\int_{a}^{b} |v(t)| \, dt$
##### Example(s):
$v(t)=t^{2}-t-6$
- displacement from $t=1$ to $t=4$

$$
\int_{1}^{4} t^{2}-t-6 \, dt =-\frac{9}{2}
$$
- total distance from $t=1$ to $t=4$
