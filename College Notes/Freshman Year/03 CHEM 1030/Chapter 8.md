### Solution Concentration
Homogeneous mixtures are called solutions -> two parts
- *Solute*: the thing that is dissolved
- *Solvent*: thing that is doing the dissolving

Dilute: small ratio of solute to solvent

Concentrated: large ratio of solute to solvent

A common way to express concentration is **Molarity** $M$:

$$
\text{Molarity}=\frac{\text{amount of solute (mol)}}{\text{volume of solution (L)}}
$$

This **Molarity** can be used as a conversion factor between moles of the solute and liters of the solution

Dilutions are when a new solution is prepared from a stock solution and is calculated using:

$$
M_{1}V_{1}=M_{2}V_{2}
$$

##### Example(s):
If $\ce{10.0 g NaCl}$ are added to $\ce{2.0L}$ of water, what is the molarity of the solution? $\ce{NaCl molar mass: 58.44 \frac{g}{mol}}$ 

$$
\ce{10.0 g NaCl \cdot \frac{1 mol NaCl}{58.44 g NaCl} = 0.171 mol NaCl} \\
$$

$$
\ce{M = \frac{0.171 mol NaCl}{2.0 L} = 0.0856M soln.}
$$

To what volume should you dilute $\ce{0.200L}$ of a $\ce{15.0M NaOH}$ solution to obtain a $\ce{3.00M NaOH}$ solution?

$$
(15.0M)(0.200L) = (3.00M)V_{2}
$$

So $V_{2}=1.00L$. 
### Electrolytes
Materials that dissolve in water to form a solution that will conduct electricity are called *electrolytes*. Conversely, dissolved materials that will not conduct electricity are called *nonelectrolytes*.

A solution of salt (ionic) conducts electricity, however a solution of sugar (molecules) does not.

They are classified by how they dissolve in water.
- If it is dissociated into ions:
	- Strong
		- Completely dissociate into ions
		- $\ce{CaCl2(s) -> Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)}$ 
	- Weak
		- Partial dissociation into ions
		- $\ce{CHOOH(aq) -> H+(aq) + CHOO-(aq)}$ 
- Molecular compounds that dissolve in water but do not form ions are called nonelectrolytes.
