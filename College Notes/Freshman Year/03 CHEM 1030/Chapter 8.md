### Solution Concentration
Homogeneous mixtures are called solutions -> two parts
- *Solute*: the thing that is dissolved
- *Solvent*: thing that is doing the dissolving

Dilute: small ratio of solute to solvent

Concentrated: large ratio of solute to solvent

A common way to express concentration is **Molarity** $M$:

$$
\text{Molarity}=\frac{\text{amount of solute (mol)}}{\text{volume of solution (L)}}
$$

This **Molarity** can be used as a conversion factor between moles of the solute and liters of the solution

Dilutions are when a new solution is prepared from a stock solution and is calculated using:

$$
M_{1}V_{1}=M_{2}V_{2}
$$

##### Example(s):
If $\ce{10.0 g NaCl}$ are added to $\ce{2.0L}$ of water, what is the molarity of the solution? $\ce{NaCl molar mass: 58.44 \frac{g}{mol}}$ 

$$
\ce{10.0 g NaCl \cdot \frac{1 mol NaCl}{58.44 g NaCl} = 0.171 mol NaCl} \\
$$

$$
\ce{M = \frac{0.171 mol NaCl}{2.0 L} = 0.0856M soln.}
$$

To what volume should you dilute $\ce{0.200L}$ of a $\ce{15.0M NaOH}$ solution to obtain a $\ce{3.00M NaOH}$ solution?

$$
(15.0M)(0.200L) = (3.00M)V_{2}
$$

So $V_{2}=1.00L$. 
### Electrolytes
Materials that dissolve in water to form a solution that will conduct electricity are called *electrolytes*. Conversely, dissolved materials that will not conduct electricity are called *nonelectrolytes*.

A solution of salt (ionic) conducts electricity, however a solution of sugar (molecules) does not.

They are classified by how they dissolve in water.
- If it is dissociated into ions:
	- Strong
		- Completely dissociate into ions
		- $\ce{CaCl2(s) -> Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)}$ 
	- Weak
		- Partial dissociation into ions
		- $\ce{CHOOH(aq) -> H+(aq) + CHOO-(aq)}$ 
- Molecular compounds that dissolve in water but do not form ions are called nonelectrolytes.

***Writing the Ionic Equation***
- Aqueous $\ce{(aq)}$ strong electrolytes are written as ions.
- Insoluble substances, weak electrolytes, and nonelectrolytes are written in molecular form.

$$
\ce{2KOH(aq) + Mg(NO3)2(aq) -> 2KNO3(aq) + Mg(OH)2(s)}
$$

becomes...

$$
\ce{2K+(aq) + 2OH-(aq) + Mg^{2+}(aq) + 2NO3-(aq) -> 2K+(aq) + 2NO3-(aq) + Mg(OH)2(s)}
$$

The net ionic equation (which is just the ions that are involved in the formation of the precipitate) is...

$$
\ce{2OH-(aq) + Mg^{2+}(aq) -> Mg(OH)2(s)}
$$

#### Acid-Base Chemistry
***Acids***

Acids are molecular compounds that form $\ce{H+}$ when dissolved in water. Acids are composed of hydrogen, usually written first, and then one or more nonmetals, written second.

$$
\begin{array}{}
\ce{HCl, H2SO4, HNO3} \\
\ce{CH3CHOOH}
\end{array}
$$

#### Redox Reactions
Oxidation-Reduction reactions (also known as redox) are reactions in which electrons are transferred from one reactant to another.
- Many redox reactions involve the reaction of a substance with oxygen.

$$
\begin{array}{l}
\ce{4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) -> 2Fe2O3(s) : Rusting} \\
\ce{2C8H18(l) + 25O2(g) -> 16CO2(g) + 18H2O : Combustion} \\
\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(g)}
\end{array}
$$

To convert a free element into an ion, the atoms must gain/lose electrons.
- atoms that lose electrons are being oxidized
- atoms that gain electrons are being reduced

![[Pasted image 20261105095333.png]]

Oxidation and reduction must occur simultaneously. If an atom loses electrons, another atom must take them. Therefore:
- the reactant that reduces an element in another reactant is called the reducing agent (contains the element that is oxidized)
- the reactant that oxidizes an element in another reactant is called the oxidizing agent (contains the element that is reduced)

$$
\ce{2Na^0(s) + 2Cl2^0(g) -> 2Na^{+1}Cl^{-1}(s)}
$$

Therefore, $\ce{Na}$ lost $\ce{e^-}$, so it is oxidized and the reducing agent. $\ce{Cl2}$ gained $\ce{e^-}$, so it is reduced and the oxidizing agent.
