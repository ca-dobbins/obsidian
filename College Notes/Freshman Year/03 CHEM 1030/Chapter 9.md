### Energy, Work, Heat
- **Energy** is anything that has the capacity to do work
	- can be exchanged
- **Heat** is the flow of energy caused by a difference in temperature
- **Work** is force acting over distance

Energy can be classified in 2 main categories:
- **Potential Energy**: the energy stored in an object or energy associated with the composition and position of the object
- **Kinetic Energy**: the energy of motion or energy that is being transferred
	- **Thermal Energy**: the energy associated with temperature (is a form of kinetic energy)
- there are many other types including: electrical, heat/thermal, light/radiant, nuclear, chemical

The amount of energy that an object has is directly proportional to its mass and velocity. When the mass is in $\text{kg}$ and velocity is in $\frac{m}{s}$, the unit for kinetic energy is a Joule ($\text{J}$).
- $1$ joule of energy is the amount of energy needed to move a $1\text{kg}$ mass at a speed of $1 \frac{m}{s}$.

A calorie $\text{cal}$ is the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of $1\text{g}$ of water $1\degree \text{C}$.
- $1\text{kcal}$ is the energy needed to raise $1000\text{g}$ of water $1\degree\text{C}$.

The conversion between these two is $1\text{cal}=4.184\text{J}$
### Thermodynamics
- **System**: part of the universe that is the focus of study
- **Surroundings**: everything in the study that is not part of the system
Therefore, $\text{System + Surroundings = Universe}$.

Thermodynamics is the study of energy that is exchanged between the system and its surroundings.
- **Exothermic** is when energy flows from the system to its surroundings (energy of the system decreases, energy of the surroundings increases).
- **Endothermic** is when energy flows into the system from the surroundings (energy of the system increases, energy of the surroundings decreases).

***First Law of Thermodynamics***
- law of conservation of energy
	- the total amount of energy in the universe is constant, matter cannot be created nor destroyed, only converted.
	- $\Delta \text{E}_{universe}=0=\Delta \text{E}_{system}+\Delta \text{E}_{surroundings}$

**Internal Energy** ($E\;\text{or}\;U$): Sum of $KE$ and $PE$ of all the particles that compose the system.
- Kinetic Energy ($KE$): $KE=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$
- Potential Energy ($PE$): energy of position

$$
\Delta E=E_{\text{products(final)}}-E_{\text{reactants(initial)}}
$$

If $\Delta E$ is negative, energy was released from the system (Exothermic). If $\Delta E$ is positive, energy was absorbed from the surroundings (Endothermic).

Energy is exchanged between the system and its surroundings through heat and work.


$$
\Delta E=q+w
$$

This can be determined by experimentally measuring $q$ and $w$, with the easiest method keeping $w=0$. The way to do this is to use what is called a bomb calorimeter. It is used because it is able to maintain a constant volume system.

### Heat
- Heat ($q$) is the exchange of thermal energy between a system and its surroundings. This occurs when there is a temperature difference ($\Delta T$).
- heat flows from matter with high temperature to matter with low temperature until both objects reach the same temperature, known as thermal equilibrium.

**heat capacity** ($C$)
- directly proportional to its mass and the specific heat of the material
- unit is $J / \degree C$ or $J / K$

$$
q=m\cdot C_{s}\cdot\Delta T
$$

- The larger the heat capacity for an object, the smaller the temperature rise will be for a given amount of heat.

- $C_{s}$, or specific heat capacity, is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of one gram of a substance $1\degree C$.
	- Units: $J /(g\cdot \degree C)$

**heat transfer and final temperature**
- heat flows between two objects until both materials reach the same final temperature.
- the amount of heat energy lost by the hot material equals the amount of heat gained by the cold material, so:

$$
-q_{\text{metal}}=+q_{\text{water}}
$$

which becomes...

$$
-(m_{m})(C_{s_{m}})(\Delta T_{m})=(m_{w})(C_{s_{w}})(\Delta T_{w})
$$

### Work (PV)
- PV work is work caused by a volume change against an external pressure
- when gasses expand, $\Delta V$ is positive, but the system is doing work on the surroundings, so $w_{\text{gas}}$ is negative

$$
w=-P\Delta V
$$

When this is solved, we get units of $\text{atm}\cdot L$, to convert we use:

$$
101.3 J=1\text{atm}\cdot L
