### Properties
***Pressure***
- Pressure is the force exerted per unit area by gas molecules as they strike the surfaces around them
- Depends on
	- number of gas particles in a given volume
	- volume of the container
	- average speed of the gas particles

***Basic Properties***
- Pressure (P)
- Volume (L)
- Temperature (T)
	- In Kelvin (C+273)
- Amount in moles (n)
### Laws
***Boyle's Law***
- relates pressure and volume
- volume and pressure are inversely related ($P \propto \frac{1}{V}$)
	- $P_{1}V_{1}=P_{2}V_{2}$

***Charles's Law***
- relates volume and temperature
- volume and temperature are proportional ($V\propto T$)
	- $\frac{V_{1}}{T_{1}}=\frac{V_{2}}{T_{2}}$

***Avogadro's Law***
- relates volume and moles
- volume and moles are proportional ($V\propto n$)
	- $V_{1}n_{1}=V_{2}n_{2}$
#### Ideal Gas Law
The simple gas relationships can be combined into a single law.

$$
PV=nRT
$$

Where $R$ is...

$$
\begin{array}{}
0.082057 \,\text{L}\cdot \text{atm}\cdot \text{mol}^{-1}\cdot \text{K}^{-1}
\end{array}
$$
##### Example(s):
As you breathe, you inhale by increasing your lung volume. A woman has an initial lung volume of 2.75 L, which is filled with air at an atmospheric pressure of 1.02 atm. If she increases her lung volume to 3.25 L without inhaling any additional air, what is the pressure in her lungs?

$$
(1.02)(2.75)=(3.25)P_{2}
$$

So $P_{2}=.863\,\text{atm}$.

Calculate the volume occupied by 0.845 mol of nitrogen gas at a pressure of 1.37 atm and a temperature of 315 K.

$$
(1.37\,\text{atm})V=(.845\,\text{mol})(315\,\text{K})R
$$

So $V=15.9\,\text{L}$.
