### Properties
***Pressure***
- Pressure is the force exerted per unit area by gas molecules as they strike the surfaces around them
- Depends on
	- number of gas particles in a given volume
	- volume of the container
	- average speed of the gas particles

***Basic Properties***
- Pressure (P)
- Volume (L)
- Temperature (T)
	- In Kelvin (C+273)
- Amount in moles (n)
### Laws
***Boyle's Law***
- relates pressure and volume
- volume and pressure are inversely related ($P \propto \frac{1}{V}$)
	- $P_{1}V_{1}=P_{2}V_{2}$

***Charles's Law***
- relates volume and temperature
- volume and temperature are proportional ($V\propto T$)
	- $\frac{V_{1}}{T_{1}}=\frac{V_{2}}{T_{2}}$

***Avogadro's Law***
- relates volume and moles
- volume and moles are proportional ($V\propto n$)
	- $\frac{V_{1}}{n_{1}}=\frac{V_{2}}{n_{2}}$
#### Ideal Gas Law
The simple gas relationships can be combined into a single law.

$$
PV=nRT
$$

Where $R$ is...

$$
\begin{array}{}
0.082057 \,\text{L}\cdot \text{atm}\cdot \text{mol}^{-1}\cdot \text{K}^{-1}
\end{array}
$$
##### Example(s):
As you breathe, you inhale by increasing your lung volume. A woman has an initial lung volume of 2.75 L, which is filled with air at an atmospheric pressure of 1.02 atm. If she increases her lung volume to 3.25 L without inhaling any additional air, what is the pressure in her lungs?

$$
(1.02)(2.75)=(3.25)P_{2}
$$

So $P_{2}=.863\,\text{atm}$.

Calculate the volume occupied by 0.845 mol of nitrogen gas at a pressure of 1.37 atm and a temperature of 315 K.

$$
(1.37\,\text{atm})V=(.845\,\text{mol})(315\,\text{K})R
$$

So $V=15.9\,\text{L}$.
### Condition of Gases
***Standard Conditions***
- Because the volume of a gas varies with $P$ and $T$, there are a standard set of conditions so that comparison is easy. These are called **standard conditions (STP)**.
	- $\text{Pressure}=1\,\text{atm}$
	- $\text{Temperature}=273\,\text{K}=0\degree\text{C}$
	- $\text{Amount}=1\,\text{mol}$
	- $\text{Volume}= 22.4\,\text{L}$
		- the volume accepted by one mole of substance is its *molar volume* at STP

***Density***
- Density is the ratio of mass to volume, usually for gases given in $\text{g/L}$.

***Mixtures***
- Many gas samples are not pure but are mixtures of gases.
	- In certain applications, the mixture is considered one gas.
- The pressure of a single gas in a mixture is called its **partial pressure**. This can be calculated in one of the following ways:
	- A fraction of the mixture it composes and the total pressure are known, or
	- The number of moles of the gas in a container of a given volume and temperature is known.
- The sum of the partial pressures of all the gases in the mixture equals the total pressure. This is known as **Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures**.
	- $P_{\text{total}}=P_{a}+P_{b}+P_{c}+\dots$

##### Example(s):
A $1.00\,\text{L}$ mixture of helium, neon, and argon has a total pressure of $662\,\text{mmHg}$ at $298\,\text{K}$. If the partial pressure of helium is $341\,\text{mmHg}$ and the partial pressure of neon is $112\,\text{mmHg}$, what mass of argon is present in the mixture?
- to solve: find the partial pressure of $\ce{Ar}$ first, then use the ideal gas law to determine mass

$$
662\,\text{mmHg}=341\,\text{mmHg}+112\,\text{mmHg}+x\,\text{mmHg}\,|\,x=209\,\text{mmHg}
$$

$$
209\,\text{mmHg}\times \frac{1\,\text{atm}}{760\,\text{mmHg}}=.275\,\text{atm}
$$

$$
n=\frac{PV}{RT}=\frac{(.27\,\text{atm})(1\,\text{L})}{R(298\,\text{K})}=1.125\times10^{-2}\text{ mol Ar}\times \frac{39.95\text{ g}}{1\text{ mol Ar}}=.449\text{ g Ar}
$$
### Kinetic Molecular Theory
1. Gases are composed of a large number of particles that behave like hard, spherical objects in a *state of constant, random motion*.
2. The particles move in a straight line until they *collide* with another particle or the walls of the container.
3. The particles are much smaller than the distance between particles. Most of the volume of a gas is therefore *empty space*.
4. There are *no force of attraction between gas particles* or between the particles and the walls of the container.
5. *Collisions* between gas particles or collisions with the walls of the container are perfectly *elastic*. *None of the energy* of a gas particle *is lost* when it collides with another particle or with the walls of the container.
6. The average kinetic energy of a collection of gas particles *depends on the temperature of the gas and nothing else*.
