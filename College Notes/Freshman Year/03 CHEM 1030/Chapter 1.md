### Matter
*what everything is made of*

Matter is composed of particles, and how they come together dictates its physical properties. It is defined as anything that has mass and takes up space
- Atoms:
	- basic subatomic particle, fundamental building block
- Molecules:
	- particles formed from 2+ atoms
Matter is classified as:
- its state (physical form)
	- solid, liquid, gas
- its composition
	- types of molecules
*state of matter changes solid-liquid-gas as temp and pressure increases*

Matter can also be classified as:
- pure substance
	- one singular component
		- element
			- cannot be broken down further
			- ex: $\ce{He or O2}$ 
		- compound
			- composed of  $\geq$ 2 elements in a fixed proportion
			- ex: $\ce{H2O , NaCl}$ 
- mixture: varies from one sample to another
	- heterogeneous
		- varies from one region to another
		- ex: concrete or soup
	- homogeneous
		- uniform throughout sample
		- ex: saltwater, sweet tea

### Modern Atomic Theory
*wow a theory how exciting*
**Law of Conservation of Mass**
- matter is nether created or destroyed
**Law of Definite Proportions**
- All samples of a given compound, regardless of their source of how they were
prepared, have the same proportions of their constituent elements  ($\ce{H2O is always H2O}$)
**Law of Multiple Proportions**
- When two elements (call them A and B) form two different compounds, the
masses of element B the combine with 1 g of element A can be expressed as a
ratio of small whole numbers

### Varying Elements
*adding or removing subatomic particles*

**Isotopes**
All atoms of a given element have the same # of protons, but can vary how many neutrons they have
$$
\ce{{}^{A}_{Z}X} 
$$
A: Mass number
Z: Atomic Number
X: Element
*natural abundance is roughly constant*

**Ions**
Atoms can gain or lose electrons and become charged particles called ions
- positively charged are called cations
	- usually metals
	- ex: $\ce{Na+ or Na+\degree}$ 
	*loss of electron*
- negatively charged are called anions
	- usually non-metals
	- ex: $\ce{Cl- }$ 
	*gain electrons*
How many electrons in the $\ce{O^2-}$ ? *10 electrons*

### Atomic Mass
*average mass*
weighted value based on the element's natural abundance of each isotope
$$
\text{Atomic Mass} = \sum_{n}^{}(\text{fraction of isotope n) * (mass of isotope n)} 
$$
The masses of atoms and the percent abundances of isotopes of elements are measured using mass spectrometry
- technique that separates particles using their mass
##### Example(s):
$\ce{Cl-35}$ has an abundance of 75.77% and an amu of 34.97. $\ce{Cl-37}$ has an abundance of 24.23% and an amu of 36.97. Find the atomic mass.  
$$
0.7577(34.97)+0.2423(36.97)=35.45 
$$
