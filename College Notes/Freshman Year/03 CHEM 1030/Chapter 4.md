### Chemical Bonding
2+ elements combine, a new substance forms
- The same: $\ce{O2}$
- Different: $\ce{H2O}$
***Two main types***:
- ionic
- covalent
- *metallic (not usually seen)*
![[Pasted image 20260922085340.png]]

#### Ionic Bonds
- involves the **transfer of electrons** between a **metal** and a **nonmetal**
	- metal -> cation
	- nonmetal -> anion
- composed of a lattice
#### Covalent Bonds (molecular)
- involve the **sharing of electrons** between two **nonmetals**

#### Chemical Formulas
- ***Structural Formula***
	- visual picture
	- full formula -> most info
- ***Molecular Formula***
	- provides actual # of atoms in each element
	- med info
- ***Empirical Formula***
	- simplest ratio of formula
	- least info
##### Example(s):
Ionic Bond:
$\ce{Na + Cl->Na+Cl-}$

$\ce{CaF2}$ -> Ionic
$\ce{F2O}$ -> Molecular
$\ce{Al2O3}$ -> Ionic
$\ce{HCl(g)}$ -> Molecular
$\ce{CuCl2}$ -> Ionic
$\ce{CH2O}$ -> Molecular

### Energy
- when anions and cations form a structure, they release energy and form a crystal lattice
	- held by electrostatic attraction
	- ***Lattice energy***: the energy released when one mole of an ionic compound forms from free ions in the gas phase
$$
\ce{Na+(g) + Cl-(g)->NaCl(s) + energy}
$$
	- *in essence*:
		- greater charges = larger lattice energy
		- smaller radius = larger lattice energy
##### Example(s):
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\ce{LiF} & \to & \text{} \\
\ce{NaF} & \to & \text{} \\
\ce{NaCl} & \to & \text{} \\
\ce{MgO} & \to & \text{greatest lattice energy}
\end{array}
$$

### Naming Bonds
#### Ionic
There are two types that ionic bonds can be categorized in,
1. Metal forms only one type of ion -> charge remains the same while forming bond
2. Metal forms more than one type of ion -> metals that vary in charge from one compound to another
The rules for naming ionic compounds are as follows:
- the metal is named first and it keeps its elemental name
- if the metal can have more than one charge, report it as a roman numeral
- non-metal is named second
- add -ide to the root of the non-metal
$$
\begin{array}{c} \\
\text{Formula Unit} & | &  \text{Name} \\
\ce{NaCl} & | & \text{Sodium Chloride} \\
\ce{AlCl3} & | & \text{Aluminum Chloride} \\
\ce{FeS} & | & \text{Iron (II) Sulfide} \\
\ce{CuO} & | & \text{Copper (II) Oxide}
\end{array}
$$
***Polyatomic Ions***
These are named the same as simple ions, except that the name of the polyatomic ion is used:
$$
\ce{NaNO3->\text{Sodium Nitrate}}
$$
![[Pasted image 20260924090127.png]]
#### Covalent
compounds of non-metals are made of individual molecule units

Rules for naming:
1. use prefix for both elements
2. the first element keeps its elemental name
3. add -ide to the root of the second element
4. name elements in the same order that they are written
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\text{Mono-} & 1 & | & \text{Hexa-} & 6 \\
\text{Di-} & 2 & | & \text{Hepta-} & 7  \\
\text{Tri-} & 3 & | & \text{Octa-} & 8  \\
\text{Tetra-} & 4 & | & \text{Nona-} & 9  \\
\text{Penta-} & 5 & | & \text{Deca-} & 10 
\end{array}
$$
***Electrons***
- electrons that are shared are called bonding pairs
- electrons not shared but belong to a particular atom are called lone pairs
*when two atoms share one pair of electrons, the result is a single covalent bond*
***Double and Triple Bonds***
when two atoms share two pairs of electrons, the result is a double covalent bond.
- 4 electrons
- elements that can double-bond with themselves and each other
	- $\ce{C,N,O,S,P}$ 
similarly when three pairs are shared, the result is a triple covalent bond
- 6 electrons
- elements that can triple-bond with themselves and each other
	- $\ce{C,N,O,S}$

### Molar Mass
The Molar Mass + Avagadro's # can be used to determine the # of atoms in a given mass of an element.

Calculate the Molar Mass of Glucose $\ce{C6H12O6}$:
$$
\begin{array}{}
\ce{C6:12.011*6}=72.06 \frac{g}{mol} \\
\ce{H12:4.003*12}=12.12 \frac{g}{mol} \\
\ce{O6:15.999*6}=96 \frac{g}{mol} \\
=180.18 \frac{g}{mol}
\end{array}
$$
