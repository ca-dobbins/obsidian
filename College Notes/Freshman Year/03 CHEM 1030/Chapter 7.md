### Chemical Equations
- provide info about the reaction
	- can be$\ce{(g)}$,$\ce{(s)}$,$\ce{(l)}$,$\ce{(aq)}$.
- can be used to determine weights of reactants used and products that can be made
**Law of Conservation of Mass**: the sum of the masses of the reactants must equal the sum of the masses of the products

the study of the numerical relationship between chemical quantities is called *stoichiometry*
$$
\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O}
$$
##### Example(s):
Write a balanced equation for the reaction between solid cobalt(III) oxide $\ce{Co2O3(s)}$ and solid carbon $\ce{C(s)}$ that produces solid cobalt $\ce{Co(s)}$ and carbon dioxide gas $\ce{CO2(g)}$.
$$
\ce{ 2Co2O3(s) + 3C(s) -> 4Co(s) + 3CO2(g)}
$$
Write a balanced equation for the combustion of gaseous butane $\ce{C4H10(g)}$, a fuel used in portable stoves and grills, in which it combines with gaseous oxygen $\ce{O2(g)}$ to form gaseous carbon dioxide $\ce{CO2(g)}$ and gaseous water $\ce{H2O(g)}$.
$$
\ce{ 2C4H10(g) + 13O2(g) -> 8CO2(g) + 10H2O(g)}
$$
### Stoichiometry
Coefficients in a chemical reaction specify the amounts in moles of each substance involved in the reaction (see written notes for example)

These can also be used to convert between grams (mass) of a reactant and grams (mass) of a product
$$
\ce{mass A \cdot \left( \frac{1 mol A}{molar mass A} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{mol B}{mol A} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{molar mass B}{1 mol B} \right)}
$$
##### Example(s):
How many molecules of $\ce{CO2}$ does the combustion of $\ce{5.0g}$ of $\ce{C8H18}$ produce?
$$
\ce{2C8H18(l) + 25O2(g)->16CO2(g) + 18H2O(g)}
$$

$$
\ce{5.0g C8H18 * \frac{1 mol C8H18}{114.23g }}
$$
### Limiting Reagent, Theoretical Yield, Percent Yield
*Limiting Reagent*: The Reagent that runs out first in a chemical reaction, which, therefore limits how much product is made
*Excess Reagent*: Reagents that are not fully consumed in the reaction
*Theoretical Yield*: The maximum amount of product that can be made, based on the amount of limiting reagent

*Actual Yield*: The actual amount of product made in a chemical reaction
*Percent Yield*: How much product was made relative to how much could have been made:
$$
\text{Percent Yield}\;\% =\frac{\text{Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}}\cdot100
$$
