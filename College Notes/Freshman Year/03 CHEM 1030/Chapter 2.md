### Waves
*cool moves man*
wavelength and amplitude are independant properties

***wavelength ($\lambda$)***
- distance covered by a wave
- also determines color/type (intensive property)

***amplitude***
- strongness of wave (intensity)
- measured from midpoint to peak (height)
- determines brightness (extensive property)

light travelling at the same speed will have a shorter wavelength, so they pass more frequently
- wavelength $\lambda$ and frequency $\nu$ of electromagnetic waves are inversely proportional
$$
\begin{align}
\text{long} \,\lambda = \text{low} \, \nu \\ \\
\text{short} \, \lambda = \text{high} \, \nu \\
\end{align}
$$

$$
\nu=\frac{c}{\lambda}
$$

### Wave Duality and Uncertainty
*subtitle*

particles move on in a path determined by it's velocity, position, and forces acting on it.
- because we do not know the position and velocity of an electron, we cannot predict it's path, but we can desrbile the probibility of where it will be
- *electron behavior is indeterminate*
- best thing we can do is describe a region it will be in

***Quantum Mechanics*** <- most modern understanding

Schrodinger developed a mathematical model of calculating the probibility of finding an electron within a region
 - solutions of his equations produce many wave functions
 - **Quantum Number**: one of four numbers that specifiy shape, energy, orbital orientation, and spin orientation
	 - *Principal QN (n)*: charactarizes the energy of the electon in a perticular orbital $n\geq1$, larger n = more energy + larger orbital
	 - *Angular momentum QN (l)*: determines shape of orbital. $l$ can have int values from 0 to (n-1). each $l$ is designated a letter designation:
		 - 0 = s (spherical)
		 - 1 = p (peanut)
		 - 2 = d (donut/dumbell)
		 - 3 = f (flower?)
	 - *Magnetic QN $m_{l}$*: int that specifies orientation of the orbital. goes from $-l \,\text{to}  +l$ (includes 0).
	 - *Spin QN $m_{s}$*: describes spin value (angular momentum). Values are either $+\frac{1}{2} \, \text{(spin up)}, -\frac{1}{2} \, \text{(spin down)}$ (always has one of each)
	orbitals of same n value are within the same principal shell
	orbitals of same n and l are within the same sublevel

**S Orbital**
each principal energy state has one s orbital
lowest energy state
number of nodes = (n-1)

**P Orbitals**
each principal energy state $\geq2$ has three p orbitals
	$m_{l}$ = -1,0,+1
meach of the three orbitals points along a diff axis
	$p_{x}$,$p_{y}$,$p_{z}$
second lowesr energy
one node at nuc; total of n nodes


**Orbitals are determined from mathematical wave functions** (reporting tool not a charge)
